## Section 1: History/Context

The Jeep Gladiator has a rich history that dates back to the 1960s when the first Gladiator was introduced.

However, the modern version, launched in 2019, marked a significant return to the pickup truck segment for Jeep.

Since its launch, the Gladiator has been praised for its off-road capabilities, rugged design, and versatility.

It has become a favorite among outdoor enthusiasts and those seeking a vehicle that can handle both daily driving and extreme off-road adventures.

The decision to discontinue the 2026 model year has sparked speculation about the reasons behind it, with some pointing to evolving market trends, increasing competition, and the need for Jeep to focus on other models.

The Jeep Gladiator boasts impressive technical specifications, including a powerful 3.6-liter V6 engine that produces 285 horsepower and 260 lb-ft of torque.

For those seeking more power, a 3.0-liter EcoDiesel V6 engine is also available, offering 260 horsepower and a significant 442 lb-ft of torque.

The Gladiator's suspension is designed to handle the toughest off-road conditions, with a five-link coil suspension in the front and a five-link coil suspension with a stabilizer bar in the rear.

This, combined with its Command-Trac or Rock-Trac 4x4 systems, makes the Gladiator a formidable off-road vehicle.

## Section 3: Real World Performance

In real-world driving, the Jeep Gladiator has proven to be a capable and comfortable vehicle both on and off the road.

Its powerful engine options provide ample power for towing and hauling, with a maximum towing capacity of up to 7,650 pounds.

The Gladiator's off-road prowess is unmatched, with features like the Rock-Trac 4x4 system, which includes a two-speed transfer case with a 2.72:1 low-range gear ratio, making it ideal for rock crawling and other low-speed off-road maneuvers.

Additionally, its approach and departure angles, combined with its ground clearance, allow it to navigate challenging terrain with ease.
