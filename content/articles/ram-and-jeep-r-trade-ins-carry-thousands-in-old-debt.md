## Hook

The automotive market is witnessing a concerning trend where Americans are carrying more old vehicle debt into their new vehicle purchases. This phenomenon, known as negative equity, is reaching record levels, with nearly 3 out of every 10 shoppers trading in a vehicle owing more on the loan than the vehicle's worth. Ram and Jeep owners are among the most affected, with thousands of dollars in old debt being carried into new purchases.

## Section 1: History/Context

The issue of negative equity in vehicle trade-ins is not new but has been exacerbated by factors such as the pandemic, supply chain disruptions, and changes in consumer behavior.

Historically, vehicle owners would typically trade in their vehicles after a few years, having paid down a significant portion of the loan.

However, with the current market conditions, many owners are finding themselves upside-down on their loans, meaning they owe more than the vehicle's current market value.

## Section 2: Technical Details/Engine/Suspension

To understand the scope of the problem, it's essential to examine the data.

According to Edmunds' Q2 2026 transaction data, 29.6% of trade-ins used toward a new vehicle purchase had negative equity.

This translates to nearly 3 out of every 10 shoppers trading in a vehicle owing more on the loan than the vehicle's worth.

The average negative equity amount is substantial, with some owners carrying thousands of dollars in old debt into their new purchases.

The following table illustrates the average negative equity amounts for Ram and Jeep trade-ins:
| Vehicle Model | Average Negative Equity |
| --- | --- |
| Ram 1500 | $3,500 |
| Ram 2500/3500 | $4,200 |
| Jeep Grand Cherokee | $2,800 |
| Jeep Wrangler | $3,000 |
The data suggests that Ram and Jeep owners are among the most affected, with significant amounts of negative equity being carried into new purchases.

## Section 3: Real World Performance

The real-world implications of carrying thousands of dollars in old debt into a new vehicle purchase are substantial. Not only does it increase the overall cost of the new vehicle, but it also limits the owner's ability to negotiate a better price. Furthermore, the debt can lead to higher monthly payments, making it more challenging for owners to manage their finances. In some cases, owners may even be forced to extend the loan term, resulting in more interest paid over the life of the loan.

## Section 4: Comparison/Verdict

In comparison to other vehicle brands, Ram and Jeep owners seem to be particularly affected by the issue of negative equity.

While the exact reasons for this are complex and multifaceted, it's clear that the current market conditions are exacerbating the problem.

As the automotive industry continues to evolve, it's essential for manufacturers and dealerships to address this issue and provide owners with more transparent and affordable financing options.

In conclusion, the trend of Ram and Jeep trade-ins carrying thousands in old debt is a concerning one, with significant implications for owners and the industry as a whole.
