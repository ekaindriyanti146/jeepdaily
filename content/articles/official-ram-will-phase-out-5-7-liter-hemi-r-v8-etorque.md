## Hook

The automotive world is abuzz with the latest announcement from Ram: the phase-out of the 5.7-liter HEMI V8 eTorque in the Ram 1500. This move marks a significant shift in the brand's strategy, leaving many to wonder what prompted this decision. As we delve into the history and technical details of the HEMI V8 eTorque, it becomes clear that this change is more than just a simple engine swap.

## Section 1: History/Context

The HEMI V8 has been a staple of the Ram brand for decades, with its iconic design and impressive power output.

The introduction of the eTorque mild-hybrid system in 2019 was seen as a major innovation, providing improved fuel efficiency and enhanced performance.

However, it appears that this technology has not lived up to expectations, prompting Ram to reconsider its strategy.

The 2026 model year saw the return of the 5.7-liter HEMI V8 with the eTorque system, but it seems that this was a short-lived experiment.

With the phase-out of the eTorque system, Ram is essentially going back to its roots, relying on the proven traditional HEMI V8 engine.

## Section 2: Technical Details/Engine/Suspension

The 5.7-liter HEMI V8 eTorque engine features a belt-driven motor generator unit that provides an additional 12 horsepower and 12 lb-ft of torque.

This system also enables the engine to shut off during certain driving conditions, such as coasting or braking, to improve fuel efficiency.

However, the eTorque system adds complexity and weight to the engine, which may have contributed to its demise.

In contrast, the traditional non-hybridized HEMI V8 engine is a more straightforward design, with a focus on raw power and performance.

The technical specifications of the two engines are compared in the following table:

| Engine | Horsepower | Torque | Fuel Efficiency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.7-liter HEMI V8 eTorque | 395 hp | 410 lb-ft | 20 mpg combined |
| 5.7-liter HEMI V8 (non-hybrid) | 395 hp | 410 lb-ft | 18 mpg combined |

## Section 3: Real World Performance

So, how do these engines perform in the real world? The eTorque system was designed to provide improved fuel efficiency, but its impact on performance was minimal.

The traditional HEMI V8 engine, on the other hand, is known for its exceptional power and responsiveness.

In terms of towing capacity, the non-hybridized HEMI V8 engine is expected to maintain its class-leading capabilities, with a maximum towing capacity of up to 12,750 pounds.

The eTorque system, while providing some benefits in terms of fuel efficiency, may have compromised the overall performance and capabilities of the Ram 1500.

## Section 4: Comparison/Verdict

The phase-out of the 5.7-liter HEMI V8 eTorque marks a significant shift in Ram's strategy, but it is not entirely surprising.

The eTorque system, while innovative, may have been a solution in search of a problem.

The traditional HEMI V8 engine, on the other hand, is a proven design that has earned a loyal following.

As the automotive industry continues to evolve, it will be interesting to see how Ram adapts to changing consumer preferences and regulatory requirements.

For now, it seems that the brand is sticking to what it knows best: building powerful, capable trucks that appeal to a wide range of customers.
